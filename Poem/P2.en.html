Oh Takam, I Sing to You!

Oh Takam,
Amid the northern forests,
You lie like a majestic lamb on a hillside,
Proud yet humble,
Starting the night with the chirping of crickets
And greeting the sun at dawn alongside the sparrows.

I call your name, Oh Takam!
A name that in the mouths of your people is like a sacred chant,
In mothers’ tales a lullaby,
And in the children’s running, a breeze over the rice fields.

I salute your hills:
Balaband, standing
Like an old guardian with a green hat,
And A-Ea-Sar, who whispered its mysterious story to time.
Pir-sar, giving off the scent of ancient sanctities,
And Hemend’s wheat fields that dance and sway under the sun and wind,
Right beneath the weary gaze of the hypocrites.

I remember your hall- Tekyeh - ,
Where tears and laughter come to reconcile,
Where women, men, and children,
At the threshold of altar and kindness,
Share bread and memories,
And quiet girls beneath the Zelkovas -Ezdars - recite their poems to one another,
Unaware that they themselves are poems
In a book named Takam.

And yes, I remember Seyd Baba, and his plow called Ezal,
Sowing seeds of goodwill in Takam’s fields.
And Agh-Amoo, who learned from his father
How to understand the soil and pray to the trees.
And Kel-Seyd-Agha,
Who has now passed away,
But I know he still walks in the winds at night,
And sees himself among the two Mir-Yahyas by the lantern light.

I love Takam with all its parts:
The rusty sickle still hanging on the wall,
The tinned copper ladles that turn to rice at hall - Tekyeh - feasts,
And hands, free from class and judgment,
United in a bowl of warm soup,
And the boys' football games in the schoolyard - Madreseh Serpish -,
They played well, and
I remember how they wouldn’t let me play.

Oh Takam,
You are a mother with a great spirit,
Spreading your table for children,
With bowls of soup, oven bread, and Keshtak for all
Under the plum tree - Halidar - at Nowruz.

And I,
The stateless poet,
Praise you,
With poems whose chorus
Passes through my memory where the Marne meets the Seine,
Quiet as a flowing river, yet full of breath.



June 2025
