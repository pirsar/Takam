Night Vigil

Nine years old, maybe ten,
I was a boy full of imagination,
with eyes that still feared no darkness,
for in every shadow, they saw a hidden flame,
a light carrying the whispers of the earth,
saying: everything lives, everything speaks, everything feels longing and joy.

It was night vigil. Occasionally, the cry of Gatbeva
echoed through the rice fields:
“Hey… Haaay…”
a sound that made wind and leaves shiver,
the wild boars retreated,
and the night, in its silence, awakened like a calm, deep spring.

Gatbeva, with a felt hat and bright eyes,
led me to a rice field where both humans and boars
claimed their share,
and I learned:
the earth denies no child from its table;
every drop of rain, every ear of grain, every gust of wind has its right.

Nena, our grandmother, set the table for us:
white rice like snow, a yellow fried egg like the sun,
yogurt as white as the mountains, bread still smelling of fire.
These were not merely food,
but hymn and prayer,
words flowing in the language of soil and wind,
and Gatbeva carried them all on the stalk of his wand,
like a bright, promised banner, a charming sign of the village.

We passed through the wheat fields,
ears rippling, crickets chanting,
the scent of rain-soaked earth, a prayer from the depths of the soil.
Every step of Rika carried not just himself but existence itself,
and the world trembled and shone in his small steps.

We reached Nepar in the rice field,
fire was lit,
smoke and mosquitoes, stories and laughter, all gathered in a circle,
and I, with ears full of sound and eyes full of light,
understood that suffering and joy, darkness and light,
all sing the same song;
a hymn eternally echoing in earth, wind, water, and fire,
never-ending.

Dawn broke,
birds rose,
ponds sparkled in the morning light,
and Gatbeva’s tired but steady gaze
turned the first night vigil into a celebration of existence.

Everything—bread, stories, smoke, mosquitoes, fire, ponds, rice fields, soil—
is a word in the grand book of life,
a book whose pages are inseparable,
and all sing the hymn of being;
a song endlessly sung by waves, wind, fire, and earth.

Hassan Mousavi Takami

Paris

September 2025
