The Tale of the Flower and the Heart

Let me tell you of a serene land called Takam, where lived a kind and noble old man the people called “Seyd Baba.” In the courtyard of his home bloomed a small garden, not vast, yet full of divine grace and whispers of hidden love.

The soil was modest, yet the blessings abundant;
the trees were few, but each carried a secret deep within.

The red apple beside the veranda danced in the morning breeze, redder than a lover’s lips, stealing sleep from the eyes of children with its fragrance. And the golden mountain apple—like the sun itself cradled by the tree—shed a soft, warming light upon the soul through its delicate skin.

The fig, sweeter than a promised embrace, sat quiet and smiling on its branch. Whoever tasted it felt as though they had kissed the beloved’s lips, not merely a fruit of the earth. And the pear, with its astringent flavor, stirred reflection in those who walked the path of Horia:
That which is true may not always seem sweet;
not every sweetness is delightful, nor every bitterness sorrowful.
Sometimes in bitterness lies a secret that gently opens the soul, like dewdrops on a dawn blossom.

But the tale of the grapevine and mulberry was different.
The vine, like a lover’s heart, had climbed upward and rested upon the mulberry’s shoulder, as if asleep in its beloved’s arms after centuries of longing.
And Seyd Baba, with a gentle smile and eyes like rain, would say to the children:
“See this vine? Each branch a poem, each leaf a joyful heart;
and these sweet clusters, people as they come to friendship and kindness, as they ripen.”

Yet the most wondrous of all was a solitary red rose beside the hornbeam tree, silent and unassuming.
Year after year, it bore not more than two blossoms; but whenever it bloomed, its scent descended from the heavens like an angel’s breath.
And whoever offered their soul to that fragrance would tremble with a light-filled awe and whisper without knowing:
“O Horia! Is this your scent, or the flower’s?”

I once asked Seyd Baba:
“Dear master, why does this flower bloom so rarely, yet amaze so deeply?”
He glanced from the garden to the sky and said:
“Child, there are many flowers—but the one that brings a message from beyond blooms rarely, and each blossom carries the hearts of a thousand lovers with it.
What comes from the heavens does not often grow upon the earth.
Love is not a thing sold in every shop.”

Do not know the tree by its shadow,
know it by its fruit.
And judge not the flower by color or scent,
but by the secret hidden in the soil of its heart.

If one opens the eye of the soul, even the bitter pear may teach truth,
and the rare red rose, true love.
No flower blooms without reason,
and no fragrance comes without the Beloved’s whisper.
So if ever a scent moves your soul,
know that flower bears a message from Horia.
And if you smell nothing,
it is not that there was no flower…
it was you, still asleep.
