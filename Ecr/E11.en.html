Maryem Jan Spring

Deep in the ancient, lush forests of Hyrcania, half a farsang north of Takam, the "Maryem Jan Spring" pulsed within the earth; a living gem where sunlight, like a golden playful hand, slid through the branches, adorning the leaves with a thousand shades of gold, emerald, and pearly silver. Drops of water, like dancing stars, emerged from the ground, whispering messages from the heart of the earth.

The Mamrez (maple), Toska, Mouzie (oak), and Anjilie trees, with their sturdy trunks and upward-reaching branches, were storytellers that narrated every leaf and dewdrop. One of the trees spoke with a warm trembling voice:

"Child, tread lightly, and I will tell you the thousand-year-old stories of the earth."

The wind, master of the forest’s dance, stirred the branches and leaves and said:

"Walk with me; every breeze is a bridge to a world your eyes have yet to discover."

The sunlight, with a golden laugh, beckoned me:

"Child, reach your hand toward me, and each ray of mine will open a new path in your heart."

The birds, their songs like angelic hymns, whispered together:

"Every note we sing is a key to understanding the dance of water, leaves, and soil; harmonize with us!"

I, a ten-year-old child, walked amidst this living dance. The ground beneath me trembled, and the soil and plants told me new tales. Even the shadows welcomed me with their soft movements. Along the path, a girl with a clear voice sang the first melodies; then women and other girls joined her, and branches, leaves, and birds all harmonized in the dance with us.

Finally, the spring appeared; among raspberry and velik bushes, and calm trees, shining like a living, beating heart of the forest. The water, with its gentle harmonious voice, said:

"Child, refresh your soul; each drop of me will carry a magical light through your veins."

I bent down and touched the water with my lips; its coolness and sweetness felt like the touch of an angel’s hand, and I felt the earth’s light flowing within me. The drops of water, with a light dancing laughter, said:

"You are now part of us; with every breath, we merge with you."

The women and girls, with melodies from ancient Mazandaran, began the dance of "Chakkeh Sama"; light, sound, and movement sparkled in enchanting unity. Branches bent, leaves trembled in the sunlight, and even dewdrops moved in rhythm to our music. Time stood still, and the forest grew more alive and abundant with each breath we took.

Our return was magical as well; the vessels were filled with spring water, and women and girls carried large jars on their heads, each overflowing drop becoming a jewel following the path of light. Trees bent to illuminate our way, and the wind, with a soft laugh, kept our balance. Every step became a piece of music, every glance a flame of light and life.

We, travelers of Maryem Jan Spring, returned to Takam with clear, life-giving souvenirs. Each of us quenched a family’s thirst with the cool, pure water; and I, the storyteller of this legend, still see in my heart the songs, the gleaming leaves, the wind’s whispers, and the spring’s flow. This memory is a living jewel in my soul, and with every breath, the forest and spring speak to me, and the cosmic dance of life continues—endless and eternal.
